# PR #208 review: merchant chests, correctness lens

Blind reviewer `merchant_chests:correctness`, run 2026-10-05 against PR head `3ce674451`. Findings were then checked by independent verifiers (three for critical and high, one otherwise). Severity is the verifiers' median. Back to the [synthesized report](../README.md).

## Reviewer summary

Correctness review of the merchant-chest piece (f546918ec) at the PR head. The core chest flow is mostly sound: the restock clock, relock rotation through RotationSeed, the per-lock-cycle take clearance, stolen marking on get and steal, the player put guard, the aicompanion takeout guard and the boot-time world check, which I confirmed passes against the shipped room YAML (all 109 chests' merchants spawn in their rooms, and no chest name collides with an authored container). I found one real exploit: an unguarded mob `put` path, reachable by an AI companion, that pries merchant goods out of an open chest clean. That skips the take contest, the stolen mark, heat and recognition. The player `put` guard was added to stop exactly this, so this is the missing sibling. Two lower findings: Validate lets a mistyped restock/relock period through, which silently makes chests restock every minute, and the chest's balance knobs live in a separate data file rather than the config.balance mechanism. Not a bug but worth knowing: on shipped timing (RoundsPerDay 900, RoundSeconds 4) `restock_interval: 1 day` is one real hour. Every chest mints fresh gold and 1-3 goods hourly. By my estimate that is roughly 1,200 gold per hour across all chests (sum of the average stock values, about 2,446, times 0.5), plus the goods.

### F009 [medium] Mob/companion `put` into an open merchant chest spills merchant goods onto the floor unmarked, bypassing the take contest and stolen marking

`internal/mobcommands/put.go:105` · status **confirmed** · reported as high

usercommands/put.go was given an IsMerchantChest refusal precisely because overfilling a container past ContainerSizeMax spills a random item to the floor, 'which would pry its goods out without the take contest and without marking them stolen'. The mob-side Put (internal/mobcommands/put.go) has the same spill logic (lines 105-121) and no merchant-chest guard, only a lock check (line 48). The AI companion module issues exactly this mob command on the player's behalf: modules/aicompanion/actions.go:455-464 `case put:` -> m.issue(..., `put `+target+` `+ct.Name, ...), and scene.go:188-197 lists every unhidden room container, merchant chests included, as a `container` target. The PR guarded the companion's takeout (loot.go:146) but not its put. A spilled merchant good is IsMerchantGoods but StolenAt==0, so IsStolen() is false. Picking it up off the floor (TakeFloorItem) never calls MarkTaken, so the item is clean: no heat, no recognition by merchant or guards, sellable to any honest merchant at full price in the very zone it was taken from, storable and auctionable.

**Failure scenario.** Thief picks the chest lock (one watched contest) so the chest is unlocked; the chest holds 1-3 merchant goods. Player tells their AI companion to put ~8-10 junk items into the chest. Once len(container.Items) > ContainerSizeMax (shipped 10), every further put drops a random item on the floor; with 3 goods among 11, a merchant good falls out with roughly 27% chance per put, and the spilled junk can be re-put. Player `get`s the goods from the floor: no merchantChestTakeWatched contest, no MarkTaken, IsStolen()==false. They then sell them to the robbed merchant's neighbours at full price or store them. All of the chest's goods can be laundered this way after a single pick contest.

**Existing mechanism.** actions.IsMerchantChest (already used by usercommands/put.go and aicompanion/loot.go takeout)

**Suggested fix.** Refuse merchant chests in mobcommands.Put (return early when actions.IsMerchantChest(room, containerName); check for an import cycle, otherwise use merchantchests.ChestAt directly), and refuse them in the aicompanion `put` case so the companion reports the refusal. Defence in depth: have the spill path never eject an IsMerchantGoods item, or call MarkTaken on one with StolenBy 0.

### F067 [low] Chest balance knobs live in a bespoke data file instead of the config.balance mechanism

`internal/merchantchests/merchantchests.go:38` · status **confirmed** · reported as low

The chest's economy and theft tuning lives in DataFiles/merchant_chests.yaml `settings:`, read through a package-private Settings struct: gold_value_ratio, gold_spread, items_min/max, lock_base/per_doubling/min/max, perception_base/per_doubling, sleeping_perception_mult and the restock/relock intervals. Project rules route balance numbers through internal/configs/config.balance.go and _datafiles/config.yaml (390 knobs, overlay and admin tooling, absent-key semantics). The same feature reads its other numbers (BaubleStolenHeatHours, BaubleFenceBuyPct, SkillWeight, StealHiddenBonus) from GetBalanceConfig. Its tuning is therefore split across two systems, and these knobs are invisible to the config tooling and its override path.

**Failure scenario.** An operator retunes theft economics through the config overlay or admin config tooling and cannot find the chest gold ratio or sleeping-merchant perception multiplier. A config-only retune leaves the chest half untouched, and the two halves (heat and fence cut against chest gold and locks) drift apart.

**Existing mechanism.** internal/configs/config.balance.go + _datafiles/config.yaml Balance section (configs.GetBalanceConfig)

**Suggested fix.** Move the numeric tunables into Balance (for example a MerchantChest* group in config.balance.go with validation) and keep merchant_chests.yaml for content only: the chest roster, names, descriptions and lookout mobs.

### F068 [low] Validate accepts any non-empty restock/relock period; a typo silently makes every chest restock (mint gold and goods) every minute

`internal/merchantchests/merchantchests.go:158` · status **confirmed** · reported as medium

Validate only checks that settings.restock_interval is non-empty and does not check relock_interval at all. dueRound uses gametime.GetDate(last).AddPeriod(s.RestockInterval). AddPeriod falls over to `g.RoundNumber + qty` rounds for any unit it does not recognise (gametime.go: `// Failover to rounds return g.RoundNumber + uint64(qty)`), and to `g.Add(qty,0,0)` hours for a bare token. A typo like `1 dya` therefore produces a 1-round interval with no error or warning. Tick looks every 15 rounds, so every chest would be emptied and refilled with fresh minted gold and goods once a minute. An empty relock_interval makes AddPeriod return the open round itself, so a picked chest relocks at the next tick. The loader is otherwise strict (UnmarshalStrict, panics on bad data), so this hole is inconsistent with its own contract.

**Failure scenario.** An author retunes merchant_chests.yaml to `restock_interval: 2 dyas` (or `12 hrs`, where `hrs` takes the round failover as well). Boot passes validation. Each chest's due round becomes last+2 rounds, so Tick (every 15 rounds) restocks all 109 chests every minute: about 60x the intended gold/goods faucet, with nothing in the logs.

**Existing mechanism.** gametime.PeriodLength (gametime.go, just below AddPeriod) gives a period's length in rounds

**Suggested fix.** In Validate, require both intervals to parse to a sane length: for example gametime.PeriodLength(s.RestockInterval) at least some floor such as 1 game hour, relock_interval non-empty, and the unit token one of the recognised words. Or parse once at Load and store rounds.
