# PR #208 review: ai companions, security lens

Blind reviewer `ai_companions:security`, run 2026-10-05 against PR head `3ce674451`. Findings were then checked by independent verifiers (three for critical and high, one otherwise). Severity is the verifiers' median. Back to the [synthesized report](../README.md).

## Reviewer summary

Security review of the AI companion overhaul (Waystone Hollow courtship, a roster with one of each companion, partings, companion bauble search and butchering, the show seam, starting kits, consent changes). I found no gold or item duplication. Starting kits are taken back by ItemId when a companion is sent back. Companion bauble rolls draw on the owner's per-room ration. Salvage is gated by loot rights and HasLoot. Steal, attack and charm against the waiting companion are blocked by CheckPlayerHarm. The waiter's speech is passed through cleanText, which removes semicolons, and EscapeAnsiTags. The admin commands are admin-flagged. There are six real findings. The most serious: under the shipped default (RequirePlayerKey: true), every Hollow interview goes through the visitor's own browser relay, and the server takes whatever JSON comes back as the model's verdict. Any player can therefore forge "offer" and then "join" and claim any free companion, including the progress other players trained into it, in two calls. The other five findings: (1) the Hollow interview skips the server-key moderation that the road path applies; (2) the Hollow prompt skips relaySafeLines, which leaks other players' look descriptions from minds that were never wiped after the migration reclaim; (3) an admin grant records consent for the player, who never agreed; (4) "companion-ai off" is ignored for the Hollow when RequireConsent is false, even though the command says otherwise; (5) incidental speech while alone in a Hollow room becomes global consent.

### F029 [medium] Admin grant records AI consent on the player's behalf

`modules/aicompanion/commands.go:313` · status **confirmed** · reported as medium

bondTo now calls consentByCompanionship(owner.UserId) unconditionally, so any bond creates consent, including the admin-only `aicompanion grant <character> <profile>`, which bonds a companion to a player who never visited the Hollow or saw the sign. consentHolders does the same for every roster owner on each module load. Consented is the global flag that consentLedger hands to the model door. From then on, everything that player says near their companion, and everything their companion sees naming others, is written to her mind and sent to the model, although the player never agreed. Only a prior explicit 'companion-ai off' prevents it.

**Failure scenario.** An admin grants Mara to a new player as a favour, with RequirePlayerKey off so the server key pays. The player never saw the Hollow board or typed 'companion-ai on', yet bondTo sets rec.Consented = true and everything they say to her is posted to OpenAI. The status line that used to flag 'NOT CONSENTED' now shows nothing.

**Existing mechanism.** The explicit consent paths that already exist: the Hollow sign (showSign / hollowMayTalk) and 'companion-ai on'

**Suggested fix.** Record consent only in recruit() (the player spoke to her after the sign), not in bondTo. For admin grant, leave consent unset and show the player the sign text or tell them to run 'companion-ai on'. Drop consentHolders, or limit it to records whose SignRead is true.

### F031 [medium] Hollow courtship verdict is fully forgeable through the player's own relay: any player can claim any one-of-a-kind companion in two calls

`modules/aicompanion/hollow.go:732` · status **confirmed** · reported as high

With RequirePlayerKey true (shipped default, config.yaml), route() never returns routeServer, so dispatchHollow's call (applyRoute at line 582) always goes routeRelay to the visitor's own browser. exchangeOnce (openai.go ~251-271) takes the raw body the browser posts back and decodes it as the model reply; applyInterview parses it straight into Interview and acts on iv.Verdict and iv.Opinion. The 'two rules that hold whatever the model says' are checked only against state that the same forged reply moves: Gains++ (line 729) happens BEFORE the verdict switch, so a single forged reply with opinion +2 and verdict 'offer' sets Gains=1 and OfferedAt; the next forged reply with verdict 'join' passes considerJoining (gainsBefore>=1, offer standing, opinion above the AbandonBelow/2 floor) and recruit() runs. The code itself concedes relay replies are forgeable (runtime.go ~656 'A reply from a player's own key is forgeable by that player anyway'), but here the forged reply gates a scarce shared resource: there is one of each companion on the server, each carries roster Kept progress trained by previous owners, and a holder keeps her up to ReleaseAfterDays (60) with LastSeen refreshed on any login. The forged speech lines are also spoken by a shared world NPC in a public room.

**Failure scenario.** A malicious player opens the web client, enables their Companion key, and patches relay.js (or answers the relay websocket by hand) to return {"verdict":"offer","opinion":{"trust":2,"respect":2,"affection":2},...} and then {"verdict":"join",...}. They say two lines to Liesl in her Hollow room and leave with her, plus whatever skills earlier owners trained into her. With five colluding accounts they hold all six companions, logging in once every 59 days to keep the claims alive, and lock every other player out of the feature.

**Suggested fix.** Do not let a relay-routed reply decide anything that grants a shared resource. Either (a) never accept 'offer' or 'join' from routeRelay replies, and gate recruitment on server-side evidence (time spent courting, items actually shown via handleShow, a minimum number of distinct real-time sessions); or (b) run the verdict as a separate server-key judgment call. At minimum, count Gains only from earlier calls (compare against the value at dispatch, not after this reply's own opinion delta), require a real-time cooldown between offer and join, and cap how long a claim may be held.
