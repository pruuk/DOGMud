# PR #208 review: merchant chests, security lens

Blind reviewer `merchant_chests:security`, run 2026-10-05 against PR head `3ce674451`. Findings were then checked by independent verifiers (three for critical and high, one otherwise). Severity is the verifiers' median. Back to the [synthesized report](../README.md).

## Reviewer summary

Security review of the merchant chest piece (f546918ec, read at PR head 3ce674451). Most paths are covered: get and steal mark chest goods as stolen, player `put` and companion takeout are blocked, crafting, salvage, storage, auction and offer all honour heat, and stacks keep stolen and clean copies apart. I found three gaps. (1) A sibling path the PR fixed for players is still open for AI companions: a companion's `put` into an open merchant chest overflows it and drops a chest good on the floor. That good is never marked stolen, so it carries no heat, no recognition risk and sells for the full honest price. (2) The new take and pick contest (WatchMerchantChest) reuses the steal observer pass but not the steal mechanism around it. It skips the 60-second skullduggery cooldown and the rank-2 gate, and a caught thief is penalised only when the merchant is present. With the merchant away, the take can be retried every round, training skullduggery on each loss, and an alt bystander can make sure each attempt loses. (3) `plant` was not guarded the way `put` was: it reaches merchant chests, even locked ones, and the next restock silently deletes whatever was planted.

### F010 [medium] The merchant chest take contest skips the steal cooldown and rank gate, so it can be spammed every round and farmed for skullduggery with an alt

`internal/actions/merchant_chest.go:98` · status **confirmed** · reported as medium

WatchMerchantChest reuses stealObserverPass but not the rest of the steal mechanism (theftAttempt). That mechanism enforces the skullduggery rank-2 gate (steal.go:546, `if t.rank < 2`) and arms the shared StealCooldown when the contest runs (theftAttempt.score, steal.go:209-212, shipped at 60 real seconds). WatchMerchantChest instead calls thiefScore directly, checks no cooldown, and calls AwardResolved on every take contest, win or lose. On the usercommands side, a caught thief is refused only for the rest of the current round (merchantChestCaughtThisRound, keyed to util.GetRoundCount()). When the chest's merchant is absent (owner == nil, line 124), being caught costs nothing beyond being revealed, and the chest stays open. A thief can therefore retry `get gold <chest>` every 4-second round instead of every 60 seconds, collecting a ProgressionFailureFraction (0.35) skullduggery award on each loss. A non-party alt with high Perception standing in the room spots each attempt on purpose. It also lets a rank-0 character take from a chest someone else opened, which `steal from <chest>` would refuse.

**Failure scenario.** The thief picks a merchant chest open while the merchant is out on its schedule. An alt (not in the thief's party) with high Perception waits in the room. The thief sends `get gold chest` once per round. Each attempt loses to the alt in stealObserverPass, awards skullduggery at the failure fraction, reveals the thief and nothing else. The next round the caught key no longer matches and the thief tries again. That is roughly 15 times the training rate the 60-second StealCooldown allows for any other theft, with no crime recorded and no lock change.

**Existing mechanism.** theftAttempt (newTheftAttempt / score()) in internal/actions/steal.go, the one place that owns the skullduggery cooldown and rank gate for steal and plant

**Suggested fix.** Route the chest take and pick contests through theftAttempt: check CooldownReady(skullduggeryCooldownKey), apply the rank gate, and arm the cooldown with t.score(), as stealFromContainer does. Alternatively, make a catch with the merchant absent relock the chest (rotating the seed) so repeat attempts cost a fresh pick.

Farming therefore only works through repeated losses, which is exactly the scenario described. This is a training-rate exploit and an inconsistency with the shared steal mechanism, not a data-safety issue, so medium fits.

### F011 [medium] AI companion `put` overflows an open merchant chest and spills an unmarked chest good, bypassing the take contest and the stolen mark

`modules/aicompanion/actions.go:453` · status **confirmed** · reported as high

The PR knows that overfilling a merchant chest pries its goods out. It blocks player `put` (usercommands/put.go:61-67, "Filling one past ContainerSizeMax spills a random item on the floor, which would pry its goods out without the take contest and without marking them stolen"). It also blocks companion takeout (modules/aicompanion/loot.go). The companion `put` verb is not blocked. It issues `put <item> <container>` for any unhidden room container (scene.go:188-198 lists every unhidden container, merchant chests included). That runs mobcommands.Put, which only refuses a locked container. Past ContainerSizeMax it removes a random item and drops it on the floor (mobcommands/put.go:105-118). The only items spared are those named in room.SpawnInfo, and chest goods are never in SpawnInfo. A chest good that lands on the floor has StolenFrom set but StolenAt=0, so IsStolen() is false. MarkChestGoodsTaken is called only in the container branch of Get (get.go ~577), never in the floor branch. The good is therefore clean: no heat, no owner or guard recognition, and honest merchants buy it at the normal price in the same town.

**Failure scenario.** The thief picks a merchant chest open (ContainerSizeMax is 10 in config.yaml, and the chest holds 1-3 goods). The thief's companion carries 8 or more junk items, and the owner tells it to put them in the chest. Each put past 10 items drops a random one of the 11 onto the floor, so a chest good is likely to drop within a few puts. The thief runs `get <good>` from the floor. merchantChestTakeWatched never runs, MarkTaken never runs, and IsStolen() stays false. The good sells at the full honest ShopBuyRatio in the same heat area, and merchants and guards never recognise it. Planted junk is wiped at the next restock, so the loop costs nothing but junk.

**Existing mechanism.** actions.IsMerchantChest, the guard the PR already added to usercommands/put.go and aicompanion/loot.go

**Suggested fix.** Put the merchant chest refusal into mobcommands.Put itself, or at least into the companion `put` verb, so every mob put path inherits it. Better still, have syncContainer or Restock leave untaken goods in a state that cannot be clean outside the chest. For example, treat IsMerchantGoods() && StolenAt==0 as stolen wherever it is found outside its chest, or call MarkTaken on any item a spill removes.

### F069 [low] `plant` can put items into a merchant chest, even a locked one, and the next restock deletes them

`internal/actions/plant.go:349` · status **confirmed** · reported as low

The PR closes `put` for merchant chests and adds a locked check to stealFromContainer, but plantInContainer has neither guard. It refuses only IsSealedShut and never checks container.Lock.IsLocked() or IsMerchantChest. Restock then sets `c.Items = nil` and so destroys anything in the chest that did not come from a restock, including a player's planted item. This is an inconsistency between sibling paths rather than a value-creation exploit, because planting adds no overflow spill and taking the item back is watched. It does let a player put items into a locked chest, and anything they plant is silently deleted.

**Failure scenario.** A player runs `plant ring chest` in a merchant's shop while the chest is locked. The plant succeeds (no lock check), and the ring is wiped without notice at the next restock (restock.go `c.Items = nil`). The player loses the item, and players learn they can put things into locked merchant chests.

**Existing mechanism.** actions.IsMerchantChest refusal (usercommands/put.go) and the locked-container refusal added to stealFromContainer

**Suggested fix.** In plantInContainer, refuse locked containers and merchant chests, matching put and stealFromContainer.
