# The inner orbit: novel passages for the logo workshop

Source: `novel/what_the_moons_keep.md` (8,234 lines). `L` numbers are that
file's lines. Quotes are exact, including the novel's own dashes; `[...]` marks
a cut. Section 4 carries the book's biggest reveals, so skip it if you want to
stay unspoiled.

## Background

Gaius has three moons, and the Temple's founding Recitation calls them the Three
Witnesses. Everyone is taught that three is the whole count. Older sources
disagree: a children's skipping rhyme, the fisher folk's telling, and the margins
of ancient maps all count four, and in the end the three in the sky are shown to
have once been four.

The evidence is a symbol called **the inner orbit**. It sits at the heart of the
Temple's own emblem, carved on lintels and pressed into wax seals, and for
thousands of years people have read it as a spiral. It is four nested arcs, and
the fourth drifts off the pattern. Three arcs are the moons that stayed, and the
drifting fourth is the one that came down. The book's working line for it is
"Three in the sky. The fourth on the disc." The story bible locks that form
(`novel/STORY_BIBLE.md` L958-988).

## What the text gives you to draw

- **Four arcs struck from one center.** The innermost is tight, the next two step
  outward at even intervals, and the fourth rides wider.
- **The fourth arc drifts.** It is "slightly off-true", "drifts away at the end",
  and leaves the pattern "tangentially".
- **One small raised point along each arc.** The bible reads these as station
  positions.
- **It is a geometry and should read as one.** "A geometry, not a picture.
  Something that could be copied, carved, quoted." The hand-copied map versions
  vary in line but keep the form, so it survives rough rendering.
- **It can be mistaken for a spiral** at a glance, which is its whole history.
- **Light, when it wakes:** "The color of morning sun through a leaf", and on the
  hillside "like coals under ash". Before that the disc glows blue.
- **Left open by the book:** how far each arc sweeps, which way the set opens, and
  where on its arc each point sits.

**The important constraint:** four versus three is never two versions of the
symbol. Every appearance has four arcs, and the bible forbids a modified version
(L985-988). The 3 or the 4 lives in a number written *beside* the symbol on the
maps, and in the gap between the symbol and the sky. A logo that shows three moons
against four arcs is true to the book. A three-arc mark is not.

**Where the text disagrees with itself:**
- Where the symbol sits on the disc: main face (L1740, L6963), edge (L4185, L4613,
  L6761) or back (L7003).
- Margin numbers: "three, four, or one" (L2398), then "Three or four" (L3443). The
  "one" is never picked up again in the passages below.
- The odd moon on the ancient display: the closest and irregular (L1386), or
  distant, small and whole (L3959). The bible flags this as a viewing-position
  difference (L834-839).
- Three further marks around the disc's edge (L964, L1740) go unexplained.

## 1. What the symbol looks like

**Ch 9, Maren, the fullest description (L1740)**

> The markings she'd always taken for decoration. Three of them around the edge [...] The symbol on the main face resolved the same way up close: not a spiral. A set of nested arcs, four of them, struck from a single center — the innermost tight, the next two stepping outward at even intervals, the fourth riding wider and slightly off-true, as though whatever had drawn them had let the last one drift. Along each arc, a small raised point. A geometry, not a picture. Something that could be copied, carved, quoted. The central indentation she'd taken for damage was not a dent but a recess, shaped too deliberately for anything accidental.

**Ch 20, Maren describes it aloud (L4183-4185)**

> "Describe the symbol," Renner said.

> "Four arcs, nested. The outermost one runs off the line the others share — drifts away at the end. It's on the edge of the disc. It matches the inner orbital path on pre-crash charts."

**Ch 21, Aldric sees the disc (L4613)**

> [...] he could see the inner orbit symbol on its edge clearly in the lamplight — the four nested arcs, the raised points along their paths, the fourth arc drifting tangentially off the pattern. Exactly as on the door above the Temple of Confluence. Exactly as on the margin of a map twelve of which sat in Brennan's workroom in Greenford.

**Ch 3, Aldric first sees past the spiral (L636-640)**

> The inner spiral of the symbol — the oldest part, the part that predated the standard Chrysalis iconography by some margin, which scholars took to have been absorbed from earlier regional traditions — was not quite a spiral. He had always read it as one. Up close, with fresh attention, it was a set of concentric arcs: concentric, measured, each a deliberate interval from the center. Not a spiral at all.

> A set of orbital paths.

> The sign of the change-within-change. He had stood beneath it and blessed the newly-opened for thirty years. It had been a map of the sky the whole time.

**Ch 6, Davan at the temple lintel (L1094-1096)**

> Carved into the stone lintel in the old formal style, big enough to read from the courtyard. He'd looked at this symbol in some form his whole life — above the village shrine, on travelling priests' vestments, pressed into the wax of documents — and had always read the inner spiral as a spiral.

> He looked at it now and something in him answered — not his eyes, not his mind, but the part that had flinched from the eel-seller's anger. The symbol felt like something. Not sacred, not decorative. Lonely. The loneliness of a thing going round and round, held in place by something it couldn't leave and couldn't reach. Not a descent. A vigil.

**Ch 17, the hand-drawn map versions (L3439)**

> In each map's margin it was rendered slightly differently — different hands, different implements, different degrees of haste — but the form was consistent. A path around a center. Something in motion around something fixed.

**Ch 27, Maren finds it on the hill (L6757-6763)**

> Three of them first. Three nested arcs, shallow, faint under the grass, following the curve of the hillside's surface as it went down into the ground.

> She counted.

> Not three. Four. The fourth arc was further out, drifting from the three, the drift she had looked at on her own disc's edge for three years and had thought was a flaw in the casting [...]

> The inner orbit symbol. On the hill.

**Ch 27, at the recess (L6897)**

> [...] was the same symbol. The inner orbit. Four arcs. The fourth drifting off-true.

**Ch 27, the symbol lights (L6963)**

> Along the symbol on its face — along the four nested arcs, along the drifting fourth — a faint light had come up. Not blue. [...] Warmer. The color of morning sun through a leaf, held at its brightest for a breath and then not fading but *settling* — the color finding its working register and staying there.

**Ch 28, Davan watches the hillside (L7003)**

> The hillside was lit where the four arcs lay, in the same warmer color but very faint, like coals under ash from a night fire that had been banked for a long time and was, now, being asked a question about whether it was still a fire.

## 2. Three versus four in the old tellings

**Prologue, the Recitation, the official three (L17, L27)**

> And the Three Witnesses moved through the dark, and they saw the world below, and they said: here. Here is where it begins.

> The Three Witnesses remain above us still. They do not leave. They have never left.

**Prologue, the Irongate Recounting (L37-41)**

> Three gods fell. That is how it started.

> The second, third, and fourth gods did not fall. They would not fall. They circled the sky where the first had fallen and they circle it still, and sometimes when the light is right you can see they are angry.

**Prologue, the Tidemark Telling (L61-65)**

> Four ships. One of them came down. Not a good landing. A bad one.

> The other three ships — they didn't leave. Think about that. Every sailor knows what a ship does when she loses one of her own at sea. You don't leave. You stay. You circle. You call their name into the water until the water answers or it doesn't.

> That's what they did. They circled. They called.

**Prologue, the children's counting rhyme (L77-83)**

> *Four fires in the sky,*

> *one came down to die,*

> *three stayed up to cry,*

> *now you're born and so am I.*

**Ch 1, Maren (L210)**

> [...] the rhyme that came to her uninvited, *four fires in the sky, one came down to die*, which had four in it where the Recitation had three, an arithmetic that had never once come out even.

**Ch 13, the Fathers' Boats tradition (L2374)**

> In the oldest versions, the boats were not metaphorical. They were specific: four of them, arriving from elsewhere, three of which remained. The fourth was described variously as fallen, broken, gone into the ground.

**Ch 13, Aldric on the rhyme (L2376)**

> The children had been singing the count the theology had quietly revised, and no one had noticed because no one listens to what children are singing.

## 3. Three versus four in the evidence

**Ch 7, Aldric counts the shapes on the ancient display (L1366-1376)**

> But one — small, blue-green, third from the star — had more markings around it than the others. More notation. And, on this most recent visit, something he had not noticed before or which had not been visible before: four small shapes in tight orbit around it. Small. Regular. Arranged at different distances.

> He counted them several times. Four.

> He had grown up, as everyone had grown up, with three moons. The three Witnesses.

> He heard them now and the meaning shifted under him like a floor settling — *They do not leave.* Not faithfulness. Grief. The stubborn circling of something that would not abandon what it had lost.

**Ch 7, the odd one out (L1386-1390)**

> The four small shapes around the blue-green world. The one closest — the one that would be largest in the sky — had a quality he had not noticed before, or was only now in a position to notice: it was different from the others. Less regular in shape, as though something had happened to it.

> Then he thought about what would have to happen to a moon to make it irregular.

**Ch 13, the map margins (L2396-2404)**

> The inner orbit. The symbol from the temple doorway, isolated from the Chrysalis surround, the thing that wasn't quite a spiral.

> Beside it, in each map, a number. Always one of three: three, four, or one.

> *Three moons,* Aldric thought. *And the maps that count four are the old ones.* He did not finish the arithmetic. He had become practiced, these past weeks, at not finishing it.

**Ch 15, Edvar shows Davan two maps (L2839-2843)**

> On the third Thursday, Edvar showed him two maps. Both old, both with the margin notation. Both with different numbers beside the symbol — one with three, one with four.

> "Different sources," Edvar said. "Possibly different dates. I can't be certain." He looked at the maps. "The one with four is older. Significantly, I think. The one with three was made — I believe — after something changed."

**Ch 16, Davan tells Maren; Maren counts the sky (L3275, L3371-3373)**

> "Something changed from four to three. The symbol is the same geometry as the moons' orbit. Three moons."

> [...] the moons were rising over the eastern roofline, one by one, in their slow patient arcs. Three of them.

> Three moons. And the number used to be four.

**Ch 17, Brennan's twelve maps (L3443-3445)**

> "Three or four. In the oldest maps, four. In the most recent, three." [...] The maps showing four are all older than this. Every map showing three is younger."

> The symbol in its margin was drawn in a steadier hand than most, the number beside it a clear and careful three.

**Ch 19, Edvar's display (L3951, L3959-3961, L3991)**

> "The fourth shape is consistent across every source I've found that predates the Quiet Century. After that period, every source shows three. The transition is clean. [...]

> The fourth shape on the face was not damaged. Not from this angle, this recording. It was simply distant and small, a body on a wide orbit, far enough out that the instrument or eye that had made this record could barely resolve it. But it was there. It was whole. It was in the sky.

> And then it was not.

> The face still turned between them, the four shapes in their ancient, patient orbits, and outside the shop the real sky held three.

**Ch 20, Renner's margin correction (L4171)**

> [...] The standard astronomical tables list four major orbital bodies. The notation — in what appears to be a contemporary hand, not a later addition — marks the number as wrong. Changes four to three."

**Ch 21, Aldric (L4311)**

> Three where the oldest maps counted four. Three where the face in the undercroft had shown him four.

## 4. What it turns out to mean (SPOILERS)

**Ch 24, Aldric says it aloud (L5623-5625)**

> "The moons," Aldric said. "Three in the sky. The fourth on the disc. Four arcs. Three stations and a — " the word caught. He let the catching stand. " — and a descent." He said the word like a man who had known for weeks it was the right word and had only just now said it aloud. "The fourth arc drifts off. I had taken it for damage in the engraving. I do not take it for damage now."

> "It was meant to drift," Davan said quietly.

**Ch 27, the symbol is the hull (L6773-6777)**

> The symbol was not on the hill.

> The symbol *was* the hill.

> The material of the hull had been marked at its making with the diagram of the mission it had been part of, and the mark had been there before any hand from this country had ever thought of making a mark [...]

**Ch 29, the symbol as a letter in the mission's name (L7453)**

> [...] a four-letter unit appeared repeatedly, the first letter of which was the inner-orbit figure itself, doing the work of a letter the way a figure did the work of a letter when a language had begun in a pictogram and moved on from the pictogram without forgetting that the pictogram had once been the word.

**Ch 29, the crew manifest (L7479, L7489)**

> An oblong body, with a small notation at one extremity that resolved, when his eye adjusted, into the figure of the descent line — the drift of the fourth arc from the carving on the hillside outside, rendered here at the scale of a column entry, doing the work of a location-marker. The fourth symbol was a place.

> *Three for station, one for descent.*

**Ch 29, the three that stayed (L7499-7505)**

> *The three that remained were not survivors. They were on post.*

> They had gone to their assigned places and had stayed there because staying was their work.

**Ch 30, the last look up (L8166)**

> Three moons, full-bright together, over the eastern hills. The ridge of the country ran under them in a long dark line. The sky between them was the dry pale blue of the eastern weather, and the moons were each what they were and were also, as she looked, three of a thing.
