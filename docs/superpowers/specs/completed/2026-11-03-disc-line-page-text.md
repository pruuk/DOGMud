## What a reader holding them all learns

A hollow man hid his nature, ran in the night, passed Stillwater, and went east with a word about a disc that opens something.
A surveyor saw a clean line and a surface that was not land, and filed a report that says nothing; a cairn and cleared ground mark the way.
The hill is the wrong shape, something is under it, a room of kept things waits, and a strap and a satchel lie out there with nobody named.
Four became three, and a mark on every old map counts them; something came down, three stayed, and someone told those three to hold their post.
The moons are something, the Chrysalis was not what the priests say, and the unchanged have a dry word for them. The stones finish each broken word.

## Pages to look at first

1. **Page 2, A Salt-Stiff Page of a Sailor's Telling.** The word "ship" stays in the sailor's comparison, which is the whole misdirection. If it reads to you as giving the secret away, cut "what a ship does" to a hole.
2. **Page 25, A Burned Page About the Chrysalis.** "Soil and air and water" and "died of it" stay. It leans on the gaps to say the opposite of the truth, and a sharp reader may see through it.
3. **Page 30, A Page Addressed to Three.** Almost whole, and the most moving of the set. A reader who has also seen pages 1 and 29 can get close to the moons by themselves.
4. **Page 13, A Damp Page About a Hillside.** The tear falls just before where the man is, but "didn't walk out" tells a reader he never came back. The quest's own text says that much, and no bones are named.
