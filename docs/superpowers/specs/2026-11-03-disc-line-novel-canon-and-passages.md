# What the Moons Keep: canon on the disc, the mark, the man who went east

Source: `novel/what_the_moons_keep.md` (8,234 lines). Outline: `novel/what_the_moons_keep_outline.md` (275 lines, marked "Outline L"). Line numbers are the novel's unless marked. Quotes are in backticks, exact, and were machine-checked against the named lines. Em dashes inside quotes are the novel's.

## Bottom line for the quest

The novel backs the broad shape (a hollow man, east, a buried ship, Maren, Reth, Brennan) and contradicts several specifics. The biggest: the mark is four nested arcs, never a ring struck through with a line. There is no door and no keeper at one. The father's remains lie on the hill itself, four paces from the recess, and the last word (Ch 27) is that he never had the disc with him.

# Goal 1: canon

## 1. The disc

**Who has it.** Maren, from before Ch 1 to the end. Packing to run: L292: `Her mother's comb. The disc.`

**Where it came from.** The novel gives several versions and does not reconcile all of them.

| Claim | Line | Quote |
|---|---|---|
| Great-aunt, at fourteen | L294 | `She'd carried the disc in her coat since her great-aunt pressed it into her hand at fourteen` |
| Great-aunt, at thirteen, after the mother died | L966 | `Her great-aunt had pressed it on her after her mother died — Maren thirteen, too raw to ask anything` |
| Great-aunt, the year the mother died, girl of twelve | L6833 | `waiting for a girl of twelve who did not yet know she would be handed it the year her mother died` |
| Old family object | L1754 | `a metal disc that had been in her family for generations` |
| Father's line | L1770 | `that had sat in her father's hollow hands and his father's before that` |
| Mother's line | L2767 | `a disc passed down through three generations of hollow women` |
| Given by the father | L3085 | `One she'd been given by a father who'd left.` |
| Left by the father on purpose | L4281 | `He had left it for her because hers were the only hands in his life that had never been changed.` |

The father left when she was twelve and the mother died the following winter (L276), so thirteen fits best and fourteen is the outlier. The great-aunt is never named and appears only at L294, L966, L6833. Nearly every later reference says Maren carried it three years (L6781: `Three years of tracing it with her thumb`); one says four (L2609: `She'd held the disc four years`).

**Did the father carry it east?** Early chapters say yes, Ch 27 says no.
- Yes: L2607: `Her father had gone east with it five years ago and hadn't come back.` L4225: `Her father: he had carried this disc. He had gone east.`
- No: L6833: `The key had not been with him. The key had been west in a coal-miner's cottage in a hamlet called Ashwick, in a great-aunt's keeping`. L6781: `who had never seen the disc and the hill in the same room`. L6865: `He had me in Ashwick and the disc was with me. He came east anyway.`

Ch 27 is the scene where the truth lands, so I read it as final. Ch 30 still says it had been his (L8184: `a disc that had been her father's`), which can stand as ownership, not carrying.

**How many exist.** Never stated. Only Maren's appears. Two things bear on this:
- Reth saw plural recesses (L3599: `Recesses, shaped with a precision that suggested they were part of the original construction.`) and a mark in one (L3605: `and in one of those recesses`). By Ch 27 there is one recess (L6789: `A disc-shaped recess.`). The plural is the only opening in the text for other discs.
- Bloodline agents carry "hand-discs," a different device: L7830: `Not the inner orbit symbol; a different glyph, the one the bloodline's domestic office used for field-report.`

**What it is.** A crew ID. L7623: `the body for whom the disc functioned as a crew ID`. Outline L13 agrees: `The disc Maren's father left her is a crew ID chip.` L7623 is the only use of "crew ID" in the novel.

**What it looks like.** Inconsistent on size. L964: `Dull metal, lighter than it looked, about two fingers across.` L4257: `palm-sized, dark, the same light-swallowing material.` The novel does not call the disc grey in so many words; the nearest is L4557, where a `Smooth gray` container sits beside `smaller, palm-sized, the same material: a disc.` One side is smooth, the other has a central recess (L1740: `It was made to receive something.`). Nothing ever fills that recess; the disc itself goes into the hill's recess.

**What it does.** Opens the hill and sends a signal. The father's words: L2605: `*Going to the hill. The disc will open it.*` Seated, it starts the hull talking to the moons: L7045: `The signal was going somewhere past the hill.` The disc stays seated when she walks away: L8210: `The disc stayed seated. The symbol stayed lit.`

**How it wakes.** Nobody wakes it. There is no rite, word or procedure. It grows more active on its own, with time and with nearness to the site, in the hands of one person.
- Faint from the start: L294: `Warmer than body-warm, with the faintest vibration under her thumb`. L304: `In her pocket the disc pulsed once`.
- Fingers do nothing: L1744 to L1746: `Nothing happened.`
- A blue ring when tilted: L1750: `a thin ring of color came up at the disc's outer edge — faint, bluish, gone when she laid it flat`.
- "Waking" is Maren's own hesitant word: L2765: `a piece of metal that seemed to be waking up`.
- Rate climbs: L4207: `It started as once every few days. Now it's several times a day.`
- It answers her, not unchanged hands in general: L4273: `The disc had not answered the room. It had answered the person.`
- Davan can feel it (L3281: `she knew he'd felt it too`), but it does nothing for him.

**Near the site.**
- Five-day road, climbing count: L5321: `It was more than yesterday, and yesterday more than the day before.`
- Day five, it wakes her: L6521: `that had *woken her*, before the light had.`
- In sight of the hill the pulses merge: L6597: `Not pulsing. *Humming.*`
- At the recess: L6909: `It was warm. Almost hot.` and `The disc in her palm was a thing asking to leave her palm.`
- Seated, thumb pressed, the hum stops: L6933: `the hum in her palm that had been continuous all morning — *stopped.*`
- Then a new light: L6963: `Warmer. The color of morning sun through a leaf`.

## 2. The mark

Never a ring and line. Grep for `struck through`, `ring struck`, `ring and line`, and any "ring" within 60 characters of "line" on a line returned no mark description (same search run hit other terms, so the search works).

- The true form: L1740: `A set of nested arcs, four of them, struck from a single center — the innermost tight, the next two stepping outward at even intervals, the fourth riding wider and slightly off-true, as though whatever had drawn them had let the last one drift. Along each arc, a small raised point.`
- Its name is "the inner orbit": L2396: `The inner orbit. The symbol from the temple doorway, isolated from the Chrysalis surround, the thing that wasn't quite a spiral.`
- It is everywhere in plain sight, misread: L1094: `had always read the inner spiral as a spiral`. Temple lintels, priests' vestments, wax seals (L1094). The priests' own tattooed sign and the boundary stones use the Chrysalis spiral (L180, L998).
- In map margins with a count: L2398: `Beside it, in each map, a number. Always one of three: three, four, or one.`
- Meaning: L5623: `Three stations and a — ` then `and a descent.` A diagram of the mission.
- On the hull: L6757: `Three nested arcs, shallow, faint under the grass` then the fourth; L6775: `The symbol *was* the hill.`
- In the recess: L6897: `was the same symbol. The inner orbit. Four arcs. The fourth drifting off-true.`
- In the ship's records it is the office stamp (L7593: `the office's stamp for *mission-related*`) and a letter in the mission's four-letter name (L7453).
- Where on the disc: main face (L1740), edge (L4185: `It's on the edge of the disc.`), back (L6931: `the symbol on the back of the disc`). Inconsistent. Also L964 and L1740 mention three other marks around the edge, never explained.

## 3. Maren's father

- **Name.** None. L3265 says Maren withholds `her father's name` from Davan, and the book never gives it. She calls him `Papa` once at L6917. Grep for "father" was read in full.
- **What he was.** Hollow. L282: `He'd been hollow too — she'd always known that and never followed it anywhere.` A mender of small mechanisms: L280: `Lamp oil, and under it something metallic, sharper.` L1756: `Her father had known how to fix small precise things.`
- **Why he left.** Two motives run together. Toward the hill: L2142: `That there was something there he had to see before they caught him.` Away from pursuit, and to draw it off his daughter (Maren's guess only): L1730: `*he ran so they'd follow him and not stay to look for me.*`
- **When.** L276: `He'd left on a night like this one — windy, late autumn. She was twelve.` Five years before the book (L278, L2142). Outline L170 says ten years; the novel's text says five.
- **How far.** Through Stillwater, stopping with his relative (`my uncle`, relation otherwise unstated): L2142: `My father came through Stillwater five years ago`. Then all the way to the hill, four paces short: L6833: `he had stopped four paces short of the recess`.
- **What happened.** Unstated cause. He lay down facing the recess with nothing to put in it. L6863: `He had nothing to put in it.`
- **Remains.** Yes, found by Maren on the hill in Ch 27, on her way down to the recess (L6799: `She found him halfway there.`). No camp.
  - A pack-strap first: L6803: `A leather strap. Flat under her boot.`
  - Coat and few bones: L6809: `A coat. Wool. Once grey, now the color weather made.` and `Bones. Not many, where there should have been many.`
  - Identified by Ulla's description of the coat: L6813: `a seam along the shoulder where your grandmother mended it after the winter of the deep snow.`
  - Satchel: L6821: `A small bound book wrapped in what had been oiled cloth`, `A fire-tin with a flint. A stone.` She reads nothing and takes nothing (L6825, L6827). The book is never opened in the novel: an open hook.
  - Orientation: L6831: `The hand-line pointed at the recess.`
- **Who else knew.** A letter kept by the uncle, already opened: L2122: `It's been opened.` Vane concludes the bloodline read it: L2174: `whoever had his letter has known about it for five years`. Text wobbles on whose letter it is (L2122 from the uncle; L4229: `Ulla's letter`).

## 4. The route east

**Who walks it.** Reth, 41 years back. The father, 5 years back, no route given. The party: Maren, Davan, Aldric, Vane, Edvar with a cart. Mara, a contract man, trailing from the ford (L6243: `He had been with them since the ford.`). Journey length: L5031: `The hill is five days east.`; five or six at L2807.

**Reth's directions, the only itinerary given as directions:** L3591: `East from New Plymouth along the highland road. The turn at the third river crossing. The climb through the tree line. The bearing from the lightning-split cairn that marked the old commission station. Eleven miles northeast of that, across two gully systems and through the heavy scrub, to the exposed section of the formation.` He says the edge shows: L3593: `You'll see the edge where the scrub stops — a line, too clean, where nothing has taken root.`

**What the party passes, in order.**
1. The boatyard, then the ford, chosen over the toll gate. L5227: `The ford was gravel and a plank walkway on pilings`.
2. Old pasture road with mortarless walls, to the highland road (L5251, L5253).
3. Wayside trough at a bend (L5289). Upland heath by afternoon (L5311).
4. Night 1: dry-stone sheep-fold with hawthorns (L5397: `three walls standing and one fallen`).
5. Older walls, bracken, bedrock (L5523, L5537). A tor (L5551). Midday at a stone with a groove (L5591: `a worn groove in its top from generations of packs`).
6. Night 2: carters' stand in the lee of ash trees (L5711).
7. Spring through mossed stone (L5881), then Irongate: L5871: `a long narrow gap between two shoulders of high bedrock, running east-northeast`, with `a rust-red seam along the north shoulder`.
8. Past the gap, russet heath and old folds (L5943). Night 3: older shepherd's fold (L6303, L6413).
9. Day 4 is not narrated.
10. Day 5: a weather seam (L6513: `Dry air off dry land.`). The hill first seen over a ridge ten miles off (L6573). Five miles of heath, two gullies, tree line (L6687). Dry creek in sedge (L6695). Hunters' track to a bare stone shoulder where the cart stops (L6701). Half an hour on foot (L6715).

**Markers.** Only three in the text:
- A scratched shrine, Prologue VI (L125 to L133).
- The lightning-split cairn at the old commission station (L3591). Only in Reth's speech; the party's narrated walk never reaches it.
- The clean scrub line (L3593), plus the arcs under the grass at the hill's southeastern end (L6755 to L6761).

**The father's trail.** None exists in the text past Stillwater. No markers, no camps, no notes on the way. The first trace is the strap on the hill, seven paces upslope of the coat (L6801, L6807).

**Local name.** L792: `*the hill that moves in the light*`. It closes in the captain's log (L8124). The outline's "Old songs say not to" (Outline L19) is not in the novel.

## 5. Reth, Brennan, keepers, the door

**Reth (Ch 17).** Surveyor for the eastern territorial commission. L3547: `Forty-one years ago.` Found the ridge on his fifteenth day (L3555). Saw it at dusk (L3567). His report: L3507: `an exposed mineral deposit of uncertain composition, approximately four hundred meters in lateral extent, partially obscured by vegetation and sediment accumulation`. Filed it, then waited: L3575: `I filed the report with the commission and I waited, and nobody said anything`. Lives in Greenford, north end, in a stone cottage (L3529). Faint iridescent skin (L3531). Refuses to draw a map (L3591: `Not a map — he would not draw one`). Tells Aldric only because he is a Keeper (L3519). He saw the recess: L3599: `there is a section where the sediment has been cleared. Not by weather. By hands. Someone went there, not recently, but within the last generation.` Who cleared it is never answered (L6787 repeats it). It cannot be the father on these dates: Reth saw it 41 years ago, the father left 5 years ago. He has no link to Maren or her father in the text. Never meets her. Last seen closing his door: L3613: `I did my part,`.

**Brennan.** Scholar at Greenford, Aldric's friend of twenty years by letter (L3401). Blue door, second street above the square (L3397). Twelve maps: L3443: `Three or four. In the oldest maps, four. In the most recent, three.` Twelve years on it (L3473). The phrase `for unchanged hands` comes through his letter (L2416). He is being watched (L2430, L3481). He sends Aldric to Reth and to the mapmakers (L2434, L3645). Afraid of metal readings under the Confluence (L5765). Never meets Maren and never hears of her father in the text. A second hand in the same margins is Edvar Marrick's (L3645, L4507).

**Threshold Keeper, or any keeper at a door.** Does not exist. Grep for `Threshold Keeper` returned nothing. `threshold` hits are all the ordinary noun (e.g. L4421, L6955, L7245). "Keeper" is a church rank, held in the novel by Aldric Voss at the Temple of Confluence (L4427), and the songs use the word loosely for the unchanged (L1770: `The songs called them keepers of old things.`). Nobody guards the breach. Ch 25 and Ch 30 play on "next Keeper" (L6083, L8030). `hearth` hits are all ordinary hearths (L1680, L3863, L3957, L6059). Nearest thing to a door-watcher in spirit: Saint Imret, L4317: `a minor saint whose chief virtue had been the willingness to remain at a post that no one visited for seventy-two years.`

**The door.** There is none. L7021: `Not a door. There was no door.` It is the crash tear, overgrown, reopening once the disc is seated.
- Recess: L6897: `Circular. Sunk perhaps a quarter-inch into the surface. The size of the disc exactly.`
- Sequence: disc set, thumb pressed (L6931), hum stops, ground shifts (L6951), air changes (L6955), a sound in the bone (L6959), arcs glow (L6969), plants let go (L7007: `not torn, not pushed — *released*`), tear opens.
- It needs a hand at first: L7773: `The hill was being held open under her pad.` Later not: L8210: `Whatever had been begun did not need her thumb to continue.`
- Children's warning: L93: `*Don't touch the dark, don't touch the door,*`
- Inside: a low room of records (L7333: `They're records,`), written on one wall, voiced on the other (L7459). The captain's log plays once the moons answer (L8110). Also a further lock: the four containers under the Confluence can open only for a person with no trace of the organism (L7711), which no one alive is.

## 6. Conflicts with the game's story

Each is plain. Where a conflict depends on a game rule I did not check, I say so.

1. **The mark.** Game: a ring struck through with a line. Novel: four nested arcs with the fourth drifting (L1740). No ring and line anywhere.
2. **Waking the disc.** Game: the player wakes it. Novel: no one does; it warms by itself over years and near the site (L304, L4207, L6597). The only act is pressing a thumb on the symbol once seated (L6931).
3. **Who can use it.** Game: a player. Novel: it answers one person, not even all unchanged hands (L4273), and it is a crew ID for a body without the organism (L7623). A changed character cannot use it by the novel's logic. If DOGMud players carry the Chrysalis, this conflicts outright.
4. **How many discs.** Game: a disc the player finds. Novel: Maren's, in the family, seated for good at the end (L8210). If the game runs after the ending, that disc is in the hill. Plural recesses at L3599 are the only room for a second.
5. **The door.** Game: a door of a buried ship. Novel: `There was no door.` (L7021). A breach, not a built entrance.
6. **The Threshold Keeper.** Not in the novel in any form.
7. **Remains location and form.** Game: a trail east ending at his remains. Novel: no trail, one stop at Stillwater, remains on the hill four paces from the recess (L6833), no camp. Any waypoints or camp are new material, though nothing forbids them.
8. **The disc on the body.** If the player recovers the disc from the remains, that contradicts Ch 27 (L6833) but agrees with L2607 and L4225, so the novel argues with itself here.
9. **Why he left.** Game: after the buried thing. Novel: that, and also running from the bloodline (L2142, L1730). The game's version drops the pursuit.
10. **Who finds him.** Novel: Maren, in person (L6799). A player arriving first would pre-empt that, or if the game runs after the book, Maren would already have seen the coat and left it lying (L6827).
11. **Maren's location.** The novel has her leave Ashwick at seventeen in Ch 1 (L302). A game Maren still in Ashwick sits before Ch 1, when she knows nothing about the hill. She learns of it only at Stillwater (L2142).
12. **The cottage.** Agrees only thinly. The novel's single line: L6833 `a coal-miner's cottage`, as where the key stayed. Its only other cottage is Reth's (L3529). Maren is living with Delia the herb woman by Ch 1 (L142, L186).
13. **Reth and Brennan.** No real conflict. Reth filed a hiding report (L3507); Brennan chases margins (L3437). The novel ties neither to the father or to Maren, so any such link is new. Reth also only spoke to Aldric, after decades of silence.

**No conflict on the reveals.** The buried thing is the lander (L5919), a ship by the captain's own words (L8110). The moons are ships on station (L7505). The Chrysalis is native (L7609).

### Internal novel inconsistencies worth knowing (for choosing what to treat as canon)

- Disc age and giver (table above). Father carried it or not (above).
- Bloodline doctrine: L3287 says only unchanged hands; L4155 has Renner say the bloodline holds that objects respond to the changed. L2418 agrees with L3287.
- Hill position: L2148: `in the foothills before Irongate country`. The party's walk goes through Irongate and on (L5943).
- Hill axis: Reth `northwest to southeast` (L3555), Ch 27 `east-southeast` (L6583).
- Disc recess: L1740 main face vs L4231 underside.
- Night 3 camp: Ch 25 has them stop at the fold (L5939); Ch 26 has them pass it (L6395).
- First pulses: L304 in Ch 1, but L5321: `The first had been at the cooperage.`
- Distance: L6047 Confluence to highlands `sixty miles`; L8222 `six days' ride`; L4619 `a thousand miles of road`.
- Map dates: L3443 change about 9,000 years ago in Brennan's estimate; L3951 Edvar ties it to the Quiet Century.
- Outline L170: ten years. Novel: five.
